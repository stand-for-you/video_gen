# THE SEER OF POSSIBILITIES — story bible and full scenario (engine v0.5, 3D, 60 fps)

Source: "The Seer of Possibilities" by **Thomas O.**, creepypasta.com, posted 26 January 2015.
Told again in my own words. The page says stories "may not be narrated or performed under any circumstance" — flagged to the user; written permission needed before monetising (UNVERIFIED, not legal advice).
Target length: about **25 minutes** (long video) + a 2:45 Reel.

## 1. The story in one line
A lonely office worker does one small, harmless favour for a voice in his computer — and twelve good years later he learns that every favour was one brush stroke in a picture that ends with his own wife's death and a boy with a knife in his doorway.

## 2. Timeline (the real order of events)
1. Night 1 — Jack plays Solitaire. The internet is down. The screen goes black; **red text**. "Move the potted plant by the elevator, three inches, at 8:17."
2. Morning 1 — 8:17, empty lobby, Jack pulls the plant out three inches. Nothing happens.
3. Later that morning — his boss **James Bentley** trips on the plant and twists his ankle.
4. Night 2 — the screen: "this chain of events isn't done playing out."
5. Day 2, 3 pm — James asks **Allie** to drive him to the doctor. Ten minutes later: a truck horn, brakes, a crash.
6. The crash — Allie is killed; James survives in a coma. (SHOWN ONLY AS: a crushed car, a stopped truck, a crowd, Jack's face. No bodies, no injuries.)
7. Night 3 — the Seer explains: Allie would have cheated on him and wasted four years of his life. "There's more to come."
8. Day 3 — the office mourns. **Diego Salbara** offers Jack James' job.
9. Night 3b — a new monitor. The entity names itself: **the Seer**. It sees what will be and what can be. It has no body. It wants Jack as its paintbrush. Two promises: it will never lie, and it will never ask him to do anything that is wrong or illegal by itself. Jack types his first answer: "What do you want me to do next?"
10. Two years later — "Enter Garmin's Liquor at 12:37, a man will ask you a question, say twenty seven." The lottery man wins; two weeks later he gives Jack a cheque for ten thousand dollars. The Seer explains the man will lose everything.
11. Over twelve years — a hundred tasks. Parking space 43 at 4:47 leads, through twenty three strangers, to **Donna**, whom Jack marries.
12. The Seer's past — thousands of years old, a fortune teller and artist who painted what would come; a foolish king misread a painting, lost a battle, and had the Seer executed.
13. The last favour — "Call Riago's Pizza, let it ring three times, hang up." A pizza arrives at the wrong house. A huge, angry seventeen-year-old delivery boy. No tip. He notices the door is not locked.
14. The reveal — every task fed one chain: Allie's death ended her brother's studies, so the boy never got a good therapist; the ruined lottery man beat the boy; the mugger's money bought the drugs that made the boy's mother stop caring.
15. The end — the boy is downstairs. Donna is already gone. For the first time the Seer speaks **inside Jack's head**, in a woman's voice: without her he would have lived eighty five empty years. Jack, crying, says "Thank you."

## 4. The creature: THE SEER (monster protocol)
Every detail comes from the story: she is **thousands of years old**, she was a **fortune teller and artist who painted the future**, a king had her **executed**, she has **no body**, her voice is **feminine and calm**, she calls herself an **artist of human manipulation**, and she reaches the living only through **red text on a screen**.
- Shape: tall (2.3 m) and thin, standing very straight (not hunched like Herbert), long thin arms, very long fingers like brushes.
- Skin: pale bone-grey **painted plaster** with deep **red pigment cracks** running down it.
- Face: no eyes — two **empty sockets with red light** far back inside them. No mouth: the mouth is **painted over**, because she does not speak with a body.
- Hands: fingers dipped to the knuckle in **dry red paint**.
- Body: ancient torn robes; below the knees she simply **is not there** — she fades into the dark, because she has no body to stand on.
- Hair: long, black, hanging still even when the air moves (added in the story kit).
- She NEVER touches anything. She is only ever seen in the glow of a screen, in reflections, or in the black void behind the text.
- Motion: she breathes slowly although she has no lungs; her fingers move like a painter's; her head tilts when she is interested.

## 5. The look kit (kit q — new, different from the last three)
- **Place and era:** a modern city — a small flat with a desk and a CRT monitor, a ten-floor office tower, a lobby with two lifts, a wide street, two shops, and later a large house in the countryside. Night for everything except the office day scenes.
- **Palette:** cold grey-blue (monitor light, office glass, night) + sodium amber street lamps and warm window light + **ONE red accent: the red screen text and the blinking router light**. Blood appears twice and is dark, small and far (section 7).
- **Shapes:** flat glass, square cubicle walls, long straight corridors, round potted plant, square lift doors. Round shapes only for the plant, the pizza box and the Seer.
- **Camera habit:** the camera **pushes slowly toward glowing screens** and **orbits Jack at his desk**; wide shots crane down into the room.
- **Chapter card:** a dark monitor with a red line of text: "FAVOUR 01", "FAVOUR 02"...

## 7. Content softened (and what is shown)
- **Allie's death:** the story describes her head and body in detail. The video shows ONLY the crushed car side, the stopped truck, broken glass on the road, a crowd and the faces of the office workers. No body, no injuries. The narration says she was killed, nothing more.
- **Donna's death:** off screen. We hear a short sound, we see the living room doorway and a dark stain on the pizza box, and later the knife in the boy's hand with dark blood on it (far shot, no body, no wound). This is the story's own blood, so it is SHOWN small and dark (policy 2E) and flagged.
- **The Seer's crude line about Allie:** rewritten as "you were not in love with her, you only wanted her" — no coarse language.
- **The mugging and the beating** are told in one calm sentence each, never shown.
- No flashing: the monitor text fades in and out slowly, the police lights far away pulse slowly.
